McMillan Reservoir

Washington, DC · United States

McMillan Reservoir is part of a remarkable chapter in Washington, DC's history of public infrastructure and urban development. The site is closely connected to the Washington Aqueduct system, which was developed to provide a reliable supply of clean drinking water to the growing capital. In the early twentieth century, the McMillan Sand Filtration Plant introduced an innovative water purification system that used large sand beds to help improve the quality of the city's water. Its distinctive circular underground filtration cells and monumental architectural structures reflect the engineering ambitions of that period.

Although the original filtration operations eventually ended, the site remains an important example of historic public works and early urban planning. Its industrial character and unusual landscape offer a different perspective on Washington, beyond its better-known monuments and government buildings.
